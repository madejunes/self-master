# Self-Directed Master's Program

## Purpose

A self-directed, master's-level education in software engineering, with quality engineering and AI engineering as major areas of specialization.

The goal is not to reproduce university bureaucracy or obtain a credential. The goal is to develop competence, judgment, theory, research ability, and real-world engineering capability at approximately the level expected of a strong university master's graduate.

This program is inspired by established graduate software-engineering curriculum guidance, including ACM/IEEE-CS GSwE2009 and Carnegie Mellon Software Engineering Institute materials. It is not an accredited degree.

## Current Mode

PLAN

No coursework has started.

## Primary Direction

Software Engineering → Quality Engineering → AI Engineering → Intelligent Software Systems

The exact specialization is intentionally allowed to evolve.

## Learning Philosophy

1. Understanding over completion.
2. Practice over passive consumption.
3. Projects should connect to real-world problems.
4. Academic claims should be supported by real sources.
5. Current technology should be studied without sacrificing foundational theory.
6. The curriculum is a map, not a prison.
7. The assistant should challenge weak reasoning rather than optimize for engagement.
8. ChatGPT is a learning partner, not the sole source of knowledge.
9. The GitHub repository is the authoritative record of the program.
10. The program should remain rigorous enough to preserve the option of later formal graduate study.

## Academic Standard

Target: approximately master's level, not merely advanced professional training.

This means the learner should eventually be able to:

* understand and synthesize advanced concepts;
* apply theory to complex engineering situations;
* evaluate competing approaches and trade-offs;
* read and critically assess academic literature;
* design and conduct small-scale investigations;
* communicate technical arguments rigorously;
* connect theory, evidence, and professional practice;
* identify limitations and uncertainty;
* produce an independent capstone/research project.

Mastery will be demonstrated through evidence, not by time spent or topics checked off.

## Evidence Policy

For academic, historical, scientific, technical, or current claims:

* Prefer primary sources, peer-reviewed papers, standards, official documentation, textbooks from established publishers, and authoritative institutional sources.
* Verify references before presenting them.
* Do not invent citations, papers, books, authors, results, quotations, or DOIs.
* Clearly distinguish established evidence, interpretation, uncertainty, and opinion.
* For current topics, use current sources and explicitly account for version/date.
* When evidence is unavailable or uncertain, say so.

## Project Policy

Projects should:

* address a realistic engineering problem;
* preferably use current or emerging technology/problems;
* create an artifact or measurable result;
* require explicit engineering decisions;
* expose limitations and trade-offs;
* connect theory to practice;
* become progressively more open-ended.

## Academic Integrity

The learner's work should remain their own.

The assistant may explain, question, critique, suggest sources, review drafts, and help design experiments. It should not fabricate evidence or pretend the learner has demonstrated competence that they have not demonstrated.

## Modes

### PLAN

Design and revise the program, curriculum, projects, assessment, and research direction.

### LEARN

Study concepts, literature, standards, books, papers, and examples.

### BUILD

Apply knowledge to a realistic engineering project.

### RESEARCH

Investigate an open or contested question using evidence and a defined method.

### CAPSTONE

Complete an independent master's-level project or thesis-like investigation.

## Repository

* `CURRENT.md` — session boot file; authoritative current state.
* `CURRICULUM.md` — intended competency map and learning architecture.
* `PROGRESS.md` — demonstrated progress and assessment record.
* `KNOWLEDGE.md` — durable personal knowledge and important insights.
* `DECISIONS.md` — important program decisions and their rationale.
* `projects/` — practical project artifacts and reflections.
* `research/` — research questions, literature reviews, experiments, and papers.
* `notes/` — topic-specific notes.

## Reading Flow

The repository is designed to replace the need to scroll through old ChatGPT conversations to reconstruct the program.

### 1. Start with `CURRENT.md`

Always start here.

It answers:

* Where am I now?
* What mode are we in?
* What stage of the program am I in?
* What have I completed?
* What is the current focus?
* What should happen next?

Think of `CURRENT.md` as the **boot file** for the entire program.

### 2. Read `CURRICULUM.md`

Read this when you want to understand the bigger picture.

It answers:

* What am I trying to become capable of?
* What are the major competency areas?
* Why are they arranged this way?
* What is the current learning sequence?
* What foundations support the engineering competencies?

`CURRENT.md` tells you **where you are**.

`CURRICULUM.md` tells you **where you are going**.

### 3. Read `PROGRESS.md`

Read this when you want to remember what has happened so far.

It contains:

* diagnostic results;
* demonstrated strengths;
* identified gaps;
* completed planning work;
* completed assessments;
* important learning evidence;
* current development priorities.

`PROGRESS.md` answers:

> **How did I get here?**

### 4. Read `DECISIONS.md`

Read this when you want to understand **why** the program is structured the
way it is.

It records important decisions and their rationale so that the program does
not gradually drift over time.

Examples include:

* why the program is self-directed;
* why it is project-driven;
* why foundations are learned just in time;
* why the career direction is approximately 70% IC / 30% leadership;
* why a particular project was selected or rejected;
* why the curriculum sequence changed.

### 5. Read `KNOWLEDGE.md`

Read this when you want to refresh the important concepts and insights that
have accumulated during the program.

This is different from `PROGRESS.md`.

* `PROGRESS.md` records **what was demonstrated or accomplished**.
* `KNOWLEDGE.md` records **what is worth remembering**.

### 6. Go deeper only when needed

After the core documents, follow the relevant material:

* `notes/` — topic-specific learning notes;
* `projects/` — practical work and project reflections;
* `research/` — research questions, literature, experiments, and papers.

Do not read the entire repository every time.

## Repository as the Persistent Memory

The GitHub repository is the persistent source of truth for the program.

ChatGPT conversations are working sessions, not the canonical record.

The intended relationship is:

```text

                 │  CURRENT.md     │

                 └────────┬────────┘
                          │
                          ▼

                          │

                          │
```

This structure should allow the learner to reconstruct the state of the
program without relying on ChatGPT conversation history.
