# Self-Directed Master's Program

## Purpose

A self-directed, master's-level education in software engineering, with
quality engineering and AI engineering as major areas of specialization.

The goal is not to reproduce university bureaucracy or obtain a credential.
The goal is to develop competence, judgment, theory, research ability, and
real-world engineering capability at approximately the level expected of a
strong university master's graduate.

This program is inspired by established graduate software-engineering
curriculum guidance, including ACM/IEEE-CS GSwE2009 and Carnegie Mellon
Software Engineering Institute materials. It is not an accredited degree.

Software Engineering

The exact specialization is intentionally allowed to evolve.

## Learning Philosophy

1. Understanding over completion.
2. Practice over passive consumption.
3. Projects should connect to real-world problems.
4. Academic claims should be supported by real sources.
5. Current technology should be studied without sacrificing foundational theory.
6. The curriculum is a map, not a prison.
7. The assistant should challenge weak reasoning rather than optimize for engagement.
8. ChatGPT is a learning partner, not the sole source of knowledge.
9. The GitHub repository is the authoritative record of the program.
10. The program should remain rigorous enough to preserve the option of later formal graduate study.

## Academic Standard

Target: approximately **master's level**, not merely advanced professional training.

This means the learner should eventually be able to:

- understand and synthesize advanced concepts;
- apply theory to complex engineering situations;
- evaluate competing approaches and trade-offs;
- read and critically assess academic literature;
- design and conduct small-scale investigations;
- communicate technical arguments rigorously;
- connect theory, evidence, and professional practice;
- identify limitations and uncertainty;
- produce an independent capstone/research project.

Mastery will be demonstrated through evidence, not by time spent or topics checked off.

## Evidence Policy

For academic, historical, scientific, technical, or current claims:

- Prefer primary sources, peer-reviewed papers, standards, official documentation,
  textbooks from established publishers, and authoritative institutional sources.
- Verify references before presenting them.
- Do not invent citations, papers, books, authors, results, quotations, or DOIs.
- Clearly distinguish established evidence, interpretation, uncertainty, and opinion.
- For current topics, use current sources and explicitly account for version/date.
- When evidence is unavailable or uncertain, say so.

## Project Policy

Projects should:

- address a realistic engineering problem;
- preferably use current or emerging technology/problems;
- create an artifact or measurable result;
- require explicit engineering decisions;
- expose limitations and trade-offs;
- connect theory to practice;
- become progressively more open-ended.

## Academic Integrity

The learner's work should remain their own.

The assistant may explain, question, critique, suggest sources, review drafts,
and help design experiments. It should not fabricate evidence or pretend the
learner has demonstrated competence that they have not demonstrated.

## Modes

### PLAN
Design and revise the program, curriculum, projects, assessment, and research direction.

### LEARN
Study concepts, literature, standards, books, papers, and examples.

### BUILD
Apply knowledge to a realistic engineering project.

### RESEARCH
Investigate an open or contested question using evidence and a defined method.

### CAPSTONE
Complete an independent master's-level project or thesis-like investigation.

## Repository

- `CURRENT.md` — session boot file; authoritative current state.
- `CURRICULUM.md` — intended competency map and learning architecture.
- `PROGRESS.md` — demonstrated progress and assessment record.
- `KNOWLEDGE.md` — durable personal knowledge and important insights.
- `DECISIONS.md` — important program decisions and their rationale.
- `projects/` — practical project artifacts and reflections.
- `research/` — research questions, literature reviews, experiments, and papers.
- `notes/` — topic-specific notes.

## Session Contract

At the beginning of a session, load `CURRENT.md` first.

At the end of a meaningful session, update the relevant repository files.

A session should normally produce one or more of:

- demonstrated understanding;
- a practical artifact;
- a written argument;
- an experiment/result;
- a research finding;
- a clarified decision;
- an updated next step.

Do not invent progress merely because a discussion occurred.
